// PRACTICAL AI GOVERNANCE

An AI governance framework for Malaysian companies

A practical AI governance framework for Malaysian companies starts with the PDPA obligations that apply to the organisation, the responsible-AI principles in Malaysia’s AIGE guidelines, and any sector-specific requirements. PDPA is law; AIGE is voluntary guidance. Turn those into an AI inventory, accountable owners, data and risk assessments, approved-use rules, human-review steps, and evidence that controls work.

NovaGenAI helps organisations put that framework into operation through data mapping, governance assessments, privacy and security controls, audit logging, and ISO/IEC 42001 readiness support. We connect policies to the way your AI actually uses data, makes recommendations and takes actions, with responsibilities and review evidence defined for each deployment.

AIGE provides voluntary responsible-AI guidance alongside existing law. ISO/IEC 42001 provides an AI management-system standard. NovaGenAI’s readiness work supports preparation; certification is conducted by an independent certification body.

// OPERATIONAL GOVERNANCE

From AI policy to operating controls

An AI policy becomes useful when the team can apply it to a real workflow. For each deployment, identify the information entering the system, the decisions or actions it can make, the person accountable for the outcome and the evidence needed to review it.

Use these questions to prepare for an AI governance assessment:

  • Purpose: What business problem does this system address, and which uses are outside its approved scope?
  • Data: Which sources may it use, where does processing occur and what appears in logs or backups?
  • Access: Who can retrieve information, change the configuration and approve new connections?
  • Authority: Which actions can run automatically, which require approval and which are prohibited?
  • Quality: What examples will test correctness, source support, privacy boundaries and failure handling?
  • Human oversight: Who receives exceptions, and what happens when that person is unavailable?
  • Operations: Who owns monitoring, document updates, incident response and changes after launch?

The output should be responsibilities and controls that teams can operate, with evidence of what was tested. A documented policy, a vendor feature and a tested workflow are different forms of assurance; review how they work together for the intended use.

A voice agent needs controls for caller identity, recordings and escalation. A document assistant needs permission-aware retrieval and current sources. An action-taking agent needs scoped tools and approval gates. A Copilot rollout needs information readiness and clear employee review responsibilities.
